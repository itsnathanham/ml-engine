Promo targeting sandbox

Who should get the promo?

A promotions team has a fixed budget and one offer. This sandbox tests five ways to decide who gets it, using 20,000 made-up members where, unlike real life, we know what each one would do with and without the promo.

The offer

$3 off the fee on your next cash advance

Goal: more members take an advance in the next 14 days, without losing money.

More settings
PopulationSeed 42

Credit risk sets expected losses per advance: low-risk $0.50, medium $2.50, high $9 at "Normal." Generating new members also retrains the models.

1 · Setup

Only some members can be persuaded

What you're seeing: every member falls into one of four groups, based on how the promo changes their chance of taking an advance. In real life these labels are invisible. The models have to infer them.

Why it matters: the whole job is reaching the green group without paying discounts to the lime one. Coral members are worse off for being contacted.

Where the models learn from: last quarter's A/B test. Members were randomly split, half got the promo, half didn't.

Why it matters: that gap is the average effect. It hides big differences between people, which is exactly what targeting tries to exploit.

TermRandomized test (A/B test) Splitting members by coin flip so the only difference between groups is the promo. The only reliable way to measure cause and effect, and the training data for the models.

Go deeper: look at individual members

What you're seeing: the left columns are what the app can actually observe. The shaded columns are each member's true chance of borrowing without and with the promo. In real life you only ever see one of the two.

2 · The decision

Five ways to spend the same budget

Each strategy sends the same number of promos (2,500). The only difference is who it picks.

3 · Result

Credit and impact tell different stories

What you're seeing: the striped bar is what a typical dashboard credits to the promo: every advance taken by someone who got it. The solid bar is what the promo actually caused, meaning advances that wouldn't have happened otherwise.

TermIncrementality The extra behavior a promo causes, beyond what would have happened anyway. The solid bar.

Go deeper: who each strategy actually reached

What you're seeing: each bar is one strategy's promo list, split into the four hidden groups.

Why it matters: rules land in the right neighborhood but can't spot the pockets, like dormant members who still direct-deposit, or skip the coral group. The models learn those patterns from the test.

Go deeper: how far down the list is worth going?

What you're seeing: each line starts with the member a strategy likes best and works down its list. The height is extra advances caused so far. The dashed line is perfect knowledge. The dotted marker is your current budget.

Why it matters: a good model rises steeply early. Where a line flattens, extra budget stops buying anything. Data scientists call this a Qini or uplift curve.

4 · Result

Only some strategies make money

What you're seeing: net profit from the promo. That's revenue from the extra advances, minus expected credit losses on them, minus every discount paid (including to members who'd have borrowed anyway). Hover a bar for the breakdown.

TermNet incremental margin Profit the promo creates after its own costs. For a lender, the number that matters more than extra advances.

5 · Proving it

In real life, a holdout group finds the truth

What you're seeing: one simulated run of a real test. A random slice of the chosen members doesn't get the promo. Comparing the two groups estimates the true effect, with real noise.

Why it matters: re-run it a few times. Small holdouts give wide, jumpy ranges. Big ones are precise but withhold the offer from more members. Choosing that trade-off is a product call, not just a stats one.

TermConfidence range The span the true effect most likely falls in. About 1 run in 20 will miss it by chance.

6 · Takeaways

What carries over to the real job

01

Target the change, not the activity

"Most likely to borrow" and "most likely to borrow because of us" are different lists. Only the second is worth paying for.

02

Score in dollars, with risk inside

For a lender, the target is profit after credit losses and promo cost, not more advances.

03

Keep a holdout, always

Dashboards credit the promo for everything recipients do. A persistent holdout is the only honest comparison.

04

Rules first, then earn the model

Good rules capture the main effect. A model's edge is in the pockets rules miss and the members rules should skip.
