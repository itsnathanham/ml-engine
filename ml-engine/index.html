Meet the members

Every member falls into one of four groups, based on how the promo changes their behavior.

A sample of members

What you're looking at

The left columns are what Dave's systems can actually see: pay cadence, days since last advance, app sessions, direct deposit, risk tier. The hidden columns are each member's chance of borrowing without and with the promo.

Why it matters

In real life you only ever observe one of those two numbers per member: they either got the promo or didn't. Uplift modeling exists to estimate the gap you can't see directly.

The readout everyone wants to believe

Advances credited to the promo on a typical dashboard, versus advances the promo actually caused.

What you're looking at

Credited counts every advance a promo recipient took. Caused counts only the extra advances that wouldn't have happened without the promo. The gap is borrowing that was going to happen anyway.

Why it matters

A propensity model is built to find people likely to act, so it wins the credited metric and loses the one that matters. "Clean incrementality" in the job description is about never confusing these two bars.

Who each strategy actually reached

The mix of members behind each strategy's promo sends.

What you're looking at

Each bar is one strategy's promo list, split by the four hidden groups. Green is money well spent. Lime is discounts handed to people who'd have borrowed anyway. Coral is actively counterproductive.

Why it matters

Behavioral rules land in the right neighborhood but can't see the pockets (like dormant members who still direct-deposit) or avoid sleeping dogs. The uplift model learns those patterns from the test data.

5

The uplift curve

Extra advances caused as each strategy works down its ranked list, from best-scored member to worst.

What you're looking at

Data scientists call this a Qini curve. The dashed line is perfect knowledge of every member's true response. A good model bows up toward it early; random targeting is a straight line. The dotted marker is your current budget.

Why it matters

It answers two questions at once: how good is the model, and how far down the list is it worth going? Where a curve flattens or bends down, extra promo spend stops buying anything.

6

Follow the money

Net incremental margin: extra advances × (revenue − expected credit loss) − every discount paid out.

What you're looking at

Promo cost is paid on every advance a recipient takes, so targeting sure things burns budget. Credit losses are subtracted from the advances the promo causes, so pushing high-risk members can turn growth into loss.

Why it matters

In short-term lending, the objective isn't "most advances," it's margin after losses. Try raising the credit loss weight: the risk-aware model gets pickier, while plain uplift keeps spending on members who cost more than they return.

7

What a real holdout test would tell you

In real life there's no hidden truth. You hold back a random slice of the chosen members and compare.

What you're looking at

One simulated run of the test, with real coin-flip noise. The bar shows the 95% confidence range for extra advances, measured by comparing promo recipients to the held-back group. The marker is the hidden truth.

Why it matters

Re-run it a few times. Small holdouts give wide, jumpy ranges, and some runs miss the truth entirely. Big holdouts are precise but withhold the promo from more people. Picking that trade-off is a product decision, not just a stats one.

8

Takeaways

01

Target the change, not the outcome

"Most likely to borrow" and "most likely to borrow because of us" are different lists. Propensity finds the first; uplift finds the second.

02

Credit is not causation

Any readout that credits every recipient's activity to the promo will overstate impact. A persistent holdout is the only honest denominator.

03

Score in dollars, with risk inside

For a lender, the target is incremental margin after expected losses and promo cost, not incremental advances.

04

Rules are a fine v0

Good behavioral triggers capture the main effect. The model's edge is in pockets rules miss and members rules should skip.
